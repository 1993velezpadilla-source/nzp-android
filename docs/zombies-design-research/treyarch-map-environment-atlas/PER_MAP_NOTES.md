# Per-Map Environment Notes

These notes focus on **what to learn**, not how to reproduce copyrighted geometry.

## World at War

### Nacht der Untoten
- Three-room core; spawn is a broad downstairs room with five boarded windows.
- A sofa/debris barrier on the stairs demonstrates furniture as a hard progression gate.
- Help Room is smaller and combines Mystery Box with two windows and a second stair option.
- Upstairs reconnects the two lower routes.
- Lesson: when the map is tiny, one sofa, one stair, one window and one doorway can alter the entire horde path.

### Verrückt
- Institutional corridors and ward rooms work as repeated modular bays.
- Split spawn delays full-team regrouping and gives early spaces different emotional identities.
- Use hospital/asylum props mostly along walls: beds, carts, cabinets, fixtures; preserve corridor centerline.
- Reconnection/power gives the map an important "opened loop" moment.

### Shi No Numa
- Central two-floor structure is the orientation anchor.
- Four exterior branches terminate in distinct huts: Storage, Comm Room, Doctor's Quarters, Fishing Hut.
- Swamp/boardwalk terrain changes movement feel without needing excessive hard obstacles.
- Small huts support dense wall dressing because combat circulation is mostly around/outside them.
- Flogger/zipline-era utilities show how a transport/trap can also become a landmark.

### Der Riese
- Industrial courtyard, factory rooms, catwalks and teleporter chambers create a compact loop network.
- Teleporters live in recognizable destination rooms instead of anonymous corners.
- Generator/power equipment is framed as room-scale machinery.
- Catwalk/balcony elevation provides sightline advantage but intentionally reduces exits.
- Industrial clutter should be grouped around walls/machines; keep floor lanes readable.

## Black Ops

### Kino der Toten
- Two initial route choices wrap toward one large theater.
- Upper hall, dressing room and several connectors are intentionally tight.
- Theater is the hero combat/training room: stage, seating rubble, projector/teleporter and central platform provide identity.
- Backstage/dressing/service areas justify dense props; main theater keeps larger movement arcs.
- Strong lesson: let a spectacular large room feel larger by preceding it with compression.

### Five
- Office floor → War Room → laboratories creates a clear vertical hierarchy.
- War Room is the biggest orientation space; elevators act as risky gates between floors.
- Labs intentionally fragment into corridors/smaller rooms.
- Office/lab props should reinforce function: desks, cabinets, carts, lab benches, control panels.
- Elevators should never be treated as neutral travel; they are temporary combat commitment.

### Ascension
- Soviet launch-facility machinery provides enormous silhouettes.
- Lunar Lander pads work as both traversal and landmark destinations.
- Exterior launch areas can stay sparse because towers, rocket geometry, gantries and fences provide scale.
- Interior service spaces can carry more small clutter without compromising outdoor loop readability.

### Call of the Dead
- Lighthouse supplies a persistent vertical landmark.
- Ship, ice, water and rock create naturally different zones without relying on signage.
- Snow/ice terrain and wreckage can funnel routes with broad, organic masses rather than tiny props.
- Long-range visibility should always terminate on something memorable: lighthouse, ship, cliff, machinery.

### Shangri-La
- Moderately sized but intentionally tight; two opening directions eventually meet at power.
- Mine cart, water slide, mud maze and traps make traversal itself memorable.
- Credited environment work included custom brick/column models, a water wheel, zombie-closet dressing, flooded mine-cart track and cave set dressing.
- Lesson: architectural kit + water/terrain + custom hero props can make a small route feel exceptionally specific.

### Moon
- Area 51 acts as a contrasting staging arena.
- Griffin Station uses tunnels, labs, Receiving Bay, power areas and Biodome as distinct functional modules.
- Biodome is a deliberate open-space counterpoint to narrow metal corridors.
- Sci-fi corridors need repeated structural rhythm but unique props/lights at junctions to prevent maze fatigue.

## Black Ops II

### TranZit
- Five major fog-free destinations: Bus Depot, Diner, Farm, Power Station, Town.
- Dangerous connectors include tunnel, cornfields, roads and other off-route locations.
- Fallen signs, pipes and rebar even affect riding on the bus roof.
- Lava is both visual story and gameplay path denial.
- Lesson: environmental hazards and travel obstacles can replace doors as the main route-control language.

### Nuketown Zombies
- Two houses, garages/backyards and central street create an instantly readable near-symmetry.
- Central vehicles are large obstacle islands.
- Rubble/rocks intentionally make navigation less clean than multiplayer Nuketown.
- Random dropping perk/Pap machines make "reserved landing footprints" important.
- Suburban props: sofas, dining furniture, kitchen counters, beds, mannequins, mailboxes, bins, fences, sheds.

### Die Rise
- Two skyscrapers, an upside-down section, broken shafts and moving elevators.
- Vertical one-way movement and deadly falls are core route logic.
- Tight corridors become dangerous because retreat may require waiting for an elevator.
- Never add decorative snag props near jump gaps, elevator exits or drop landings.

### Mob of the Dead
- Cell blocks, cafeteria, infirmary, docks, roof and utility spaces each have strong prop vocabularies.
- Bars/railings create visibility through boundaries — good for tension without solid walls.
- Stairs/catwalks repeatedly expose players to multi-level threats.
- Prison clutter should hug cells/walls unless deliberately forming a defendable obstacle.

### Buried
- Western storefront silhouettes make navigation intuitive even with many interiors.
- General Store, saloon/hotel, courthouse, bank, candy shop and mansion each get distinct functional dressing.
- Example of "room function = prop set": cash register/candy barrels, buildable parts/shelving, bar furniture, domestic mansion props.
- Strong reference for our asset-tagging system.

### Origins
- Trenches are the connective tissue; generators are repeated landmark nodes.
- Excavation site is central vertical mass; church/tank station create memorable edge destinations.
- Mud is a movement modifier and therefore a route-design material.
- Giant robot footprints turn otherwise open battlefield zones into timed hazards/landmarks.
- Battlefield clutter works best as edge piles, sandbags, broken carts, crates and machinery rather than micro-collision.

## Black Ops III

### Shadows of Evil
- Junction feeds three strongly themed districts: Canals, Footlight, Waterfront.
- Each district uses signs, architecture, businesses and lighting to maintain identity.
- Breakable doors/crates and Art Nouveau assets were custom authored for the environment.
- Transit and Rift prevent the district network from becoming one long walk.
- Use repeated city kit for cohesion, then district-specific hero props for memory.

### Der Eisendrache
- Castle courtyards and ramparts give broad exterior circulation.
- Interior chambers, undercroft and stairs compress the player before opening again.
- Rocket test site creates a visually separate destination.
- Castle props: banners, suits/arms, tables, shelves, torches, masonry, machinery retrofit.
- Keep decorative protrusions on castle walls out of player collision.

### Zetsubou No Shima
- Labs A/B and bunker provide hard man-made landmarks amid organic swamp/jungle.
- Plants are not merely decoration; they participate in mechanics.
- Water/swimming/sewer routes create hidden secondary topology.
- Organic clutter can be dense visually while collision remains simplified.

### Gorod Krovi
- Ruined city blocks use destroyed facades, tram/street debris, industrial interiors and dragon-related set pieces.
- Large silhouette events keep a visually chaotic ruin readable.
- Use rubble to make broad funnels rather than hundreds of independent blockers.
- Multi-story openings let players read threats above/below without requiring full access everywhere.

### Revelations
- Fragments of older maps are separated by cosmic void/portals/jump pads.
- Nacht functions as a central orientation hub.
- Each fragment keeps recognizable material/prop identity.
- Lesson: route connectivity can be fantastical if destination identity is extremely strong.

## Black Ops IV

### Voyage of Despair
- Ship's long axis helps orientation while deck changes provide vertical complexity.
- Grand Staircase is a major anchor.
- Dining Hall tables, promenade benches, state-room furniture and machinery should be grouped according to ship function.
- Flooded cargo/engine areas create state change without changing macro geometry.
- Fast travel offsets the cost of a very long map.

### IX
- Main arena is an unusually strong central landmark and horde space.
- Danu/Ra/Zeus/Odin temple branches form clear radial choices.
- Circular underground chamber reconnects branches.
- Temple columns/statues/flames define identity while arena center remains readable.
- Excellent template for "ornate edges, clean fight center."

### Blood of the Dead
- Prison kit repeats bars, cells, catwalks and service rooms.
- Expanded exterior areas give breathing room between compressed blocks.
- Vertical sight through railings keeps the map visually connected.
- Repeated cell geometry requires lighting/color/hero props to avoid monotony.

### Classified
- Office Floor, War Room and labs preserve Five's three-level mental model.
- Developer environment-art documentation specifically notes basement lab geo cleanup, set dressing, optimization and office props.
- Excellent example of production pass: inherited/blocked geometry becomes believable through function-specific prop dressing.

### Dead of the Night
- Main Hall is the orientation reset.
- East branch: bedrooms/dining → gardens → greenhouse lab.
- West branch: billiards/library → cemetery/mausoleum.
- North: forest.
- Billiard table is a true room anchor; library uses tables/shelves; greenhouse uses large machines, workbenches and plants; crypt uses pillars/torches/stained glass.
- One of the best references for paintings, furniture, decorative rooms and gameplay coexistence.

### Ancient Evil
- Monumental Greek architecture makes columns, statuary and plazas the main large props.
- Keep temple approach routes wide; let detail sit on plinths, edges and alcoves.
- Use vertical ruins to frame skyline rather than cluttering the ground plane.

### Alpha Omega
- Familiar Nuketown surface becomes more complex through bunker/service layers.
- Surface symmetry is useful for quick orientation; underground complexity supplies depth.
- Strong reference for expanding a tiny map without erasing its original mental model.

### Tag der Toten
- Lighthouse is primary navigation beacon.
- Credited lighting work uses warm/cool contrast for direction.
- Workbenches/easter-egg items are deliberately illuminated.
- Ship bridge is brightly lit to make power easy to find.
- Booths receive tight pools of light to help locate hidden objects while preserving isolation.
- This is direct evidence that lighting belongs in gameplay authoring, not just final polish.

## Black Ops Cold War

### Die Maschine
- Nacht silhouette acts as instantly understood central landmark.
- Exterior snow/yard loops contrast with clean-ish facility/lab spaces.
- Underground tech spaces should have stronger artificial-light/signage hierarchy than surface ruins.

### Firebase Z
- Village and military facility have intentionally different prop languages.
- Teleporter gives a clean thematic transition.
- Defense sectors use broad pads/lanes that can hold large enemy groups.
- Military set dressing should frame these pads, not consume them.

### Mauer der Toten
- Dense Berlin streets need storefront/neon/sign/color landmarks.
- Rooftops, ziplines and subway produce frequent vertical state changes.
- Street props (cars, barriers, kiosks, bins) should be large/readable obstacles, not random clutter.

### Forsaken
- Theme-park/training-town-like fabricated spaces benefit from exaggerated signage and facades.
- Teleport transitions let each zone be visually distinct.
- Use "fake town" dressing deliberately: repeated facade modules with unique storefront anchors.

### Outbreak
- At large scale, micro-clutter matters less than POI silhouette.
- Ruka/Alpine/Golova launched first; later Sanatorium, Duga, Zoo, Collateral and Armada expanded region set.
- Roads, ridgelines, towers, ships and large facilities become navigation props.
- Vehicle clearance must be considered before fine dressing.

## Vanguard

### Der Anfang / Terra Maledicta
- Portal/objective structure creates a hub plus arena rhythm.
- Because players revisit hubs, landmarks must survive repeated traversal.
- Objective arenas can be denser at edges because central combat footprint must remain broad.

### Shi No Numa Reborn
- Central-spoke classic still works with modern systems because the landmark skeleton is strong.
- Modern dressing should elaborate the existing route identity rather than overcomplicate it.

### The Archon
- Useful case study in converting an objective-oriented environment to round-based expectations.
- Compare where loops, resources and holdout spaces become more important.

## Modern Warfare III Zombies

### Operation Deadbolt / Urzikstan
- Open-world scale changes prop priorities.
- POIs, roads and skyline masses dominate navigation.
- Contracts need staging areas; strongholds need readable entrances/exits; extraction needs broad readable approach.
- Treat as reference for future "large Zombies region" work, not for our first Nacht-scale map.

## Black Ops 6

### Liberty Falls
- Small-town streets tie together gas station, bowling alley, hill street and church.
- Storefront/signage and street furniture provide natural breadcrumbs.
- Cars/road furniture should form broad islands, not noisy micro-obstacles.

### Terminus
- Official guide explicitly notes many narrow hallways and doorways.
- Open island/coastal portions relieve that pressure.
- Communications/storage/mess/engineering/bio-lab each deserve distinct prop kits.
- Great reference for alternating claustrophobic facility spaces with exterior loops.

### Citadelle des Morts
- Village ascent builds anticipation before castle interior.
- Courtyard/ramparts allow height advantage.
- Sitting rooms/dining hall are ideal furniture-rich zones; dungeon/undercroft use sparse heavy masonry.
- Keep ornamental castle clutter off high-speed stair landings.

### The Tomb
- Catacomb repetition requires different shrine, ossuary, excavation and temple prop signatures.
- Bone piles, sarcophagi, columns and excavation equipment should create story islands while center routes remain readable.
- Dark Aether Nexus can act as a high-contrast visual reset.

### Shattered Veil
- Room-function navigation is explicit: Conservatory, Motor Court, Grand Foyer, Library, Study, Nursery, Director's Quarters, Bottlery, Banquet Hall.
- Use paintings/furniture/lighting to make each branch recognizable from a glance.
- Mansion rooms should feel richly dressed, but door-to-door sprint lines need clean collision.

### Reckoning
- Each Janus Tower lab function becomes its own landmark kit: quantum, mutant research, android assembly, containment, executive.
- Tech environments risk visual sameness; color/light/large-machine silhouettes should differentiate floors.
- Elevators/vertical transitions should open onto clear landing zones.

## Black Ops 7

### Ashes of the Damned
- Official design presents fractured Dark Aether locations linked by fog-covered thoroughfares.
- Ol' Tessie vehicle means road geometry/obstacle spacing is a first-class constraint.
- Node identity must be readable through fog from a moving vehicle.
- Great reference for future large original region.

### Astra Malorum
- Observatory domes, telescopes and Saturn backdrop are giant orientation anchors.
- Rusted scientific equipment supplies medium-scale dressing.
- Avoid filling observatory floors with fragile clutter; sightlines to hero equipment matter.

### Paradox Junction
- Nuketown base geometry gains time-state variation.
- Good evidence that dressing, utilities and traversal can transform familiar geometry without changing the whole footprint.
- Wall-jump access changes which roofs/vehicles become playable landmarks.

### Totenreich
- Fishing-town language: docks, boats, warehouses, homes, lighthouse-related maritime props.
- Repeated small buildings need silhouette/color/function distinction.
- Weather/Dark Aether effects should never obscure critical door/interact readability.

### Kowakujō
- Castle is structured by functional spaces such as entrance, onsen, war/storage/workshop areas.
- Ghostly Rifleman trap uses paired komainu statues at doorways — strong example of gameplay device integrated into architecture.
- Rivers of Fire are simultaneously visual, hazard and crowd-control elements.
- Doorway framing is a major prop opportunity; keep combat routes clear between architectural thresholds.

### Rex Infernus
- Starting House is a domestic-scale anchor: bedrooms upstairs; fireplace/dining downstairs.
- Transition from recognizable house proportions into corrupted stronghold amplifies surreal contrast.
- Domestic furniture should establish normality early, then supernatural architecture can break those rules later.

### BO7 Survival slices
- Vandorn Farm / Exit 115 / Zarya Cosmodrome / Mars / Ashwood / Nuked / Eidskallen Lighthouse / Tenshu demonstrate what survives when a larger environment is cropped into a confined combat arena.
- Study them for spawn pressure, resource footprint and "minimum viable landmark" density.
- Tenshu's listed areas include Entrance, Onsen Baths, War Room, Storage Rooms and Workshop — function-first room naming directly maps to prop-kit planning.
